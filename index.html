茶
Syn · thé · o, what gathers around tea

Almost five centuries ago, tea merchants were already keeping meeting notes.

In Japan, a tea gathering was a closed room, a moment prepared with care, and a record kept by someone who was there. Syntheo is built on the same three ideas.

茶会

Chakai · the gathering

A host receives guests in a closed room. Only the invited are there. Your meeting is that room, and Syntheo only talks to the servers you invite.

会記

Kaiki · the record

Afterwards, a guest writes down who came and what happened: a personal notebook, not minutes. Syntheo completes your notes; it never replaces them.

一期一会

Ichi-go ichi-e · once only

One meeting, one time. Syntheo never keeps the audio: only your note remains, sealed with 記 (“recorded”).

Read the story behind the name

One note, three moments

Like a tea gathering, a meeting has a before, a during and an after. Syntheo follows all three in the same note.

備

Prepare

Jot an agenda and a few notes, file the note in its project folder. Syntheo recalls what was said in the last meetings of the same project.

聴

Listen

It captures your microphone and your computer's audio, with a live transcript in the margin. Nobody joins the call.

結

Synthesize

Your notes become the write-up. What comes from your notes stays green; what Syntheo adds is in ink, so you always see who wrote what.

Every meeting picks up where the last one ended

File your notes in project folders. Syntheo keeps a running thread of decisions, open points and tasks, and drafts the agenda of the next meeting from it.

Your models, your servers.

Syntheo is the notepad, not the service. Point it at the speech-to-text and language models you already run, on your laptop, on a workstation down the hall, or in your data centre.

A small speech model fits on a recent laptop; a medium language model runs on an RTX 3090, a Mac with MLX or a DGX Spark.
